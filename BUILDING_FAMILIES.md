# Building Families

This file records recurring architectural families visible in Citadel references. It describes what to look for in source imagery; it does not prescribe a final layout.

## 1. Dense urban tower family
- Tall and medium-height structures packed closely together.
- Curved, cylindrical, rounded, or irregular silhouettes recur.
- Mixed rooflines create a layered skyline.
- Streets and elevated structures appear integrated with the buildings.
- Use repeated visual proportions from source frames rather than inventing universal dimensions.

## 2. Civic / government family
- Larger, more deliberate monumental spaces.
- Council/government interiors have strong central staging and controlled circulation.
- Likely requires public access, controlled access, offices, meeting rooms and security zones where shown.
- Only directly observed rooms should be treated as fixed.

## 3. Police / security family
- Administrative rooms plus training/operational spaces.
- Shooting-range and academy functions are directly associated with Citadel policing.
- Vehicle access and rapid circulation are important observable requirements.

## 4. Morty residential/commercial family
- Repeated low/medium-rise urban forms.
- Dense storefronts and ordinary street-level circulation.
- Stronger signs of crowding and lower-status infrastructure than elite Rick areas are useful visual distinctions when directly supported by shots.

## 5. Industrial family
- Larger floor plates and machinery.
- Simple Rick's factory demonstrates assembly-line production, industrial equipment and security response.
- Industrial spaces should be separated from generic urban buildings in the evidence database.

## 6. Utility / technical family
- Machinery-heavy rooms, conduits, portals, large access doors and service spaces.
- Dimensional technology requires substantially larger technical infrastructure than a handheld Portal Gun.
- Waste-disposal infrastructure is another distinct industrial family.

## 7. Commercial storefront family
- Cafes, shops, entertainment and service businesses occur inside the dense city fabric.
- Record signage, frontage, doorway count, window proportions, street relationship and visible interior depth whenever a source frame permits.

## 8. Agricultural / natural family
- Farm areas can contain trees, water, terrain and Mega Fruit/Mega Seed vegetation.
- This proves the Citadel is not uniformly metallic or fully urbanized.
- Do not flatten natural areas into generic landscaping.

## 9. Construction / repair family
- Scaffolding, unfinished structures, exposed surfaces and active crews are part of the post-damage visual state.
- Construction evidence should be tied to the specific Citadel era.

## 10. Ruin family
- Broken shell sections, debris, exposed infrastructure and damaged buildings appear after destruction.
- Ruins should be documented as evidence of what was inside the structure, not simply as decoration.

## Streetscape details to catalog from every useful frame

- road width relative to pedestrians/vehicles
- lane organization
- curb/sidewalk form
- crosswalks
- storefront setbacks
- street furniture
- signs and advertisements
- trees/planters
- fountains/water features
- pedestrian density
- conventional cars
- hover/taxi vehicles
- police vehicles
- overhead/elevated structures
- visible utility conduits
- building entrances and service doors
- vertical circulation
